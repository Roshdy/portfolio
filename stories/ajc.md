##### The Rescue Mission

In 2016, Al-Jazira Capital had a trading platform built on jQuery that had **crashed on every single launch for the better part of a year**. I was deployed as an on-site resource from E-Business Solutions for a 3-month assignment. By the end of month one, every UI requirement was completed and all backend integrations were live. In month two, we finally identified the root cause — a deep memory leak — and I resolved it within **two weeks**. The platform moved to pilot, and the reaction from both the development and business departments was extraordinary. Month three: _live trading on Tadawul_. Mission accomplished.

##### Earning a Seat at the Table

Al-Jazira Capital offered me a full-time role to stay and maintain the platform. What started as bug fixing and production support quickly evolved. Within months I was introduced to the full backend: a .NET Framework service, classic ASP.NET pages, and an Oracle database with PL/SQL I'd eventually own entirely. The business had vision and ambition — and I was determined to meet it. One of my early highlights was building a dedicated internal management system for the security team using Angular and .NET Framework 4.7, which became _one of the most adopted internal tools_ in the department.

##### The Week I Won the React Debate

I had been advocating for a technology stack upgrade for over two years — pushing to replace classic ASP and jQuery with React and .NET Core. The opportunity arrived with an online onboarding project. There was significant internal resistance: the team was unfamiliar with React, and concerns were raised about single-resource dependency. So I went home and spent **a week** building a fully working React frontend demo from scratch. I walked back in with a working product. The team moved forward with what I had built — and we were ahead of schedule on the frontend before the project formally kicked off. _React had a permanent home at Al-Jazira Capital_.

##### Designing from 200 Endpoints Up

The onboarding project brought an integration with Oracle's BMS platform for multi-department onboarding automation. I was deeply involved in the design sessions with the vendor — challenging flow logic, identifying missing failure paths, and shaping the integration architecture. The SOAP/WCF service we built on top of it was a success, and it gave React the final endorsement it needed across the organisation. From there, the business agreed to a full platform rebuild. We catalogued **200+ legacy endpoints** and organised them into business domains: Accounts, Portfolios, Profiles, Market Data, and a set of interface services for legacy systems including T24-Temenos and BPM. I introduced Ocelot as the API gateway — a single secure entry point to the frontend — and we embedded SeqLog across every service for meaningful, level-structured logging. Then we planned every task, estimated every hour, and assigned every resource in Microsoft Project. I was the _youngest on the team. I was also the team lead_.

##### Delivered. Twice. Ahead of Schedule.

We executed through the COVID-19 outbreak — fully remote, fully focused. Phase 1 of the new customer portal was delivered **three weeks ahead of schedule**. Phase 2 followed at **1.5 months early**. Agile principles were embedded throughout using GitLab Kanban boards with burndowns, heatmaps, and milestone tracking that I reported directly to the PMO. The business then commissioned a design firm to revamp the entire platform UI. I was the only developer in those rooms — contributing to feasibility assessments, vendor integration architecture, and Figma design reviews, often becoming a key voice alongside — and sometimes ahead of — the business team themselves.

##### Building What I Believed In

Some of the work I'm most proud of at Al-Jazira Capital never made it to production — not for technical reasons, but due to the organisation's conservative risk culture at the time. I built a .NET Core authentication microservice that abstracted our costly RSA/Entrust dependency and enforced CMA-mandated concurrent session rules at both the API and database layers. I collaborated with a Java-oriented colleague to redesign LightStreamer's real-time data layer — moving from rigid comma-separated strings to dynamic gzipped JSON subscriptions — **tripling server capacity from 400 to 1,300 concurrent users**. _That one shipped_. I also built POCs for price alerts using Oracle Advanced Queuing, a conditional orders engine with a full research and implementation cycle, and a Java-based parallel market price processor running directly from the exchange feed. Each was a technically sound initiative; each was shelved. After years of pushing the ceiling, I decided it was time to find an environment where the pace of innovation matched my own.
